Why picture bingo is the easiest Christmas party game

Bingo needs no rules explanation, no teams and no prep beyond printing. Picture bingo goes one step further: every square has a painted picture with its label, so kids who can't read yet can play right next to grandparents. When the adults want something a little faster, switch to the word bingo cards.

  • Family Christmas parties: one game fits everyone from age 3 to 93, and it fills the half hour between dinner and dessert.
  • Classroom holiday parties: a teacher or room parent can call the pictures while kids mark their cards.
  • Office and church gatherings: the word bingo cards are quick to play with adults and need nothing but a pen.
  • Bilingual families: the same game comes in English and in Spanish, so everyone can play in the language they like.

How to play Christmas bingo

  1. Print the cards (two picture cards per page) and cut the picture calling cards apart.
  2. Give each player a card and some markers: candy, mini marshmallows, buttons or a crayon.
  3. Shuffle the calling cards in a bowl or hat. Draw one at a time and say it out loud.
  4. Players mark the matching square. The first to complete a row, column or diagonal shouts "Bingo!" and wins.
  5. For a longer game, play blackout: the winner is the first to cover the whole card.

Printing tips

Print on plain US Letter or A4 paper at "Actual size", or on light cardstock if you want the cards to last all season. Slip a card into a sheet protector and use a dry-erase marker to reuse it round after round. Prize ideas: candy canes, hot cocoa packets, a small ornament, or first pick at the cookie tray.
