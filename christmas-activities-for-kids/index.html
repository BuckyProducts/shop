Screen-free Christmas activities kids actually enjoy

Winter break is long, and by the third day most kids have watched every holiday movie twice. Printable activities fix that without a trip to the craft store: print a few pages in the morning and you have a quiet hour while you wrap presents, cook or work from home.

  • Coloring pages: the easiest win for ages 3 to 7. Thick lines are easier for little hands, and a finished page makes a nice card for a grandparent.
  • Christmas games: charades, Would You Rather and Christmas Scattergories get the whole family playing after dinner, with no setup.
  • Mazes, dot-to-dots and word searches: perfect for early finishers in class, restaurant waits and the car ride to Grandma's.
  • Story time: read a short printable story together, then let kids color the pictures from it.
  • Stickers and crafts: print stickers on sticker paper to decorate cards, gift bags and homemade ornaments.

Ideas by age

  • Ages 3 to 5: big coloring pages, simple mazes and stickers. Keep sessions short, 10 to 15 minutes, and let them pick the page.
  • Ages 6 to 9: word searches, dot-to-dots and charades. They love playing a game they can "run" for the adults.
  • Ages 10 and up: Scattergories, Would You Rather and drawing challenges. Older kids can also help the little ones with their pages.

A simple winter break plan

  1. Print a small stack on Sunday night: two coloring pages, one game and one puzzle per child.
  2. Keep them in a folder with crayons so kids can grab an activity on their own.
  3. Save the party games for an evening when the whole family is home.
  4. Hang finished pages on the fridge or turn them into cards for teachers and neighbors.
  5. After Christmas, the Three Kings Day kit keeps the fun going until January 6.
