What is Advent?

Advent is the season of waiting and preparing for Christmas. The word comes from the Latin adventus, "arrival" or "coming." Many churches begin Advent four Sundays before Christmas, so in 2026 it starts on Sunday, November 29. Many families simply count down from December 1 to December 24 instead. Traditionally the four weeks focus on hope, peace, joy and love, and many homes light one more candle on an Advent wreath each Sunday.

How to use a daily Advent devotional with your family

  1. Pick one time and keep it. Breakfast, after dinner or bedtime all work. Ten minutes a day is enough, and doing it at the same time every day is what makes it stick.
  2. Read the verse out loud. Let a different person read each day. Younger kids can repeat a short phrase after a parent.
  3. Read the reflection and ask one question. "What does this verse tell us about God?" or "Who could we help this week?" One question gets a better conversation than five.
  4. Pray together. Use the printed prayer, or let each person add one sentence of thanks.
  5. Do the action. Each day has a small step: call a grandparent, share a snack, write a kind note. This is the part kids remember.

Tips for families with kids

  • Make it visible. Clip the day's page to the fridge or keep the printed set in a basket by the table with a candle.
  • Let kids color or draw. While an adult reads, little ones can color a Bible verse page. They listen better with busy hands.
  • Missed a day? Just keep going. Read two pages the next day, or skip ahead. Advent is about drawing near, not keeping a perfect record.
  • Hide a verse card. Tuck a Bible verse card into a lunchbox, a coat pocket or under a pillow as a little Advent surprise.
  • End on Christmas Eve. Day 24 leads into the Christmas story in Luke 2. Read it together before bed.
